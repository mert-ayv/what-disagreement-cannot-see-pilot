# Blind reader: IUI and menotropin ovulation induction, Aetna 2023-24 (old) vs Wellfleet 2025-26 (new)

Sources read: `A_aetna_2023-24.txt` (OLD), `W_wellfleet_2025-26.txt` (NEW), `ASRM_2023.txt` (ASRM, the guideline NEW names for eligibility). No other material used. Cost sharing and network tiers ignored.

## How the two versions are built

OLD sets out a closed list of eligibility gates: covered student or covered spouse/partner; a documented condition causing infertility; no voluntary sterilization of patient or partner; no infertility from natural ageing or menopause in patient or partner; a less costly covered treatment cannot work; a months-trying or donor-cycle count by sex, age and partner; an unmedicated day 3 FSH below 19 mIU/mL, tested within 12 or 6 months. It covers specialist charges for "Ovulation induction cycle(s) with menotropins" and "Intrauterine insemination", and caps "Artificial insemination" at "6 attempts maximum per policy year". It requires prior approval.

NEW has no thresholds. It covers infertility "when such infertility is the result of malformation, disease or dysfunction or a correctable medical condition otherwise covered under this Certificate", for an "Insured Person who is an appropriate candidate", with eligibility judged by ACOG and ASRM guidelines. "Ovulation induction and monitoring" and "Artificial insemination" sit in a second tier that "may be covered" if the first-tier services "do not result in increased fertility" and the service is "Medically Necessary". The Family Planning exclusions then exclude "Infertility Treatment (male or female)", including "Injectable infertility medication, including but not limited to menotropins, hCG and GnRH agonists" and "Ovulation induction and monitoring".

Two reading conflicts in NEW affect almost every item below. They are named once here and referred to by label afterwards.

- **Conflict X (benefit vs exclusion).** X1: the specific benefit grant wins and the exclusion list applies only to items the benefit does not grant. Artificial insemination is not named in the exclusion list, so X1 leaves it covered. X2: the exclusion governs as written. Its general heading "Infertility Treatment (male or female)" would then swallow the whole benefit, and "Ovulation induction and monitoring" is excluded word for word. For **artificial insemination**, X1 is clearly the more plausible reading, since X2 would empty a benefit the same document grants. For **ovulation induction and monitoring**, the two lists contradict each other directly. I find X1 more plausible (the services are covered), because the benefit grants them specifically and an ambiguous exclusion is read against the drafter, but the conflict is genuine. For the **menotropins drug**, no granting text names injectable medication, so the specific exclusion governs. The only competing reading is that first-tier "Medically appropriate Treatment of ovulatory dysfunction" includes the drug, and I find that weaker.
- **Conflict C (causation clause vs ASRM definition).** C1: the policy's causation clause ("result of malformation, disease or dysfunction or a correctable medical condition") is a hard precondition, and ASRM only decides who is an "appropriate candidate" once that precondition is met. C2: the policy adopts ASRM's definition, under which infertility is itself "a disease, condition, or status" that includes the "need for ... donor gametes ... either as an individual or with a partner", and "Nothing in this definition shall be used to deny or delay treatment to any individual, regardless of relationship status or sexual orientation." I find C1 more plausible where no medical cause is diagnosed, because the causation clause is the operative grant and ASRM is named only "to determine eligibility". Where a medical cause is diagnosed, both readings agree.

## A. Changes that apply to every patient

### 1. Baseline IUI patient who met every OLD gate
1. Who: for example, a female under 35 with a male partner, 12+ months trying, a documented ovulatory or other condition, recent unmedicated day 3 FSH below 19, no sterilization, no less costly option, fewer than 6 IUIs this policy year, treated by an infertility specialist with prior approval.
2. Old: covered. New: covered under the more plausible reading, but under new conditions. First-tier services must first "not result in increased fertility", the IUI must be Medically Necessary, and the wording is "may be covered".
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Conflict X (X1 covered; X2 not covered; X1 more plausible). Also "may be covered": either insurer discretion, or simply conditional on the medical-necessity finding. The conditional reading is more plausible, since the sentence pairs "may" with "if ... Medically Necessary".
4. Text: OLD "We will cover charges ... Intrauterine insemination". NEW "If the above infertility services do not result in increased fertility additional tests, listed below may be covered if the tests are determined to be Medically Necessary ... Artificial insemination"; exclusion "Infertility Treatment (male or female)-this includes but is not limited to".

### 2. The menotropins drug in an ovulation induction cycle
1. Who: any patient who meets eligibility and needs a menotropin (injectable gonadotropin) ovulation induction cycle.
2. Old: covered (the benefit names "Ovulation induction cycle(s) with menotropins"). New: not covered.
3. Reading-dependent: yes, but weakly. The competing reading puts menotropins under first-tier "Medically appropriate Treatment of ovulatory dysfunction". The specific exclusion of "menotropins" is more plausible. (OLD has its own ambiguity: it covers "charges made by an infertility specialist", which may not reach a pharmacy-dispensed drug. Read naturally, a cycle "with menotropins" includes them.)
4. Text: OLD "Ovulation induction cycle(s) with menotropins". NEW exclusion "Injectable infertility medication, including but not limited to menotropins, hCG and GnRH agonists".

### 3. Physician, monitoring and ultrasound services of a menotropin ovulation induction cycle
1. Who: same patient; the non-drug parts of the cycle (induction management, follicle monitoring, ultrasound, blood tests).
2. Old: covered. New: unclear. Covered under X1 (more plausible); not covered under X2. Even under X1, the cycle is only partly covered because the drug is excluded (item 2).
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Conflict X; the same phrase appears in the benefit and in the exclusion.
4. Text: NEW benefit "Ovulation induction and monitoring; Pelvic ultrasound"; NEW exclusion "Ovulation induction and monitoring".

### 4. hCG trigger and GnRH agonists used in IUI or ovulation induction cycles
1. Who: any patient whose IUI or OI cycle uses an hCG trigger shot or a GnRH agonist.
2. Old: covered as part of the cycle. The text does not mention these drugs, and the cycle "begins with the initiation of therapy". New: not covered.
3. Reading-dependent: no for NEW (named in the exclusion). For OLD, the drug-versus-specialist-charge point in item 2 applies.
4. Text: NEW "Injectable infertility medication, including but not limited to menotropins, hCG and GnRH agonists". OLD cycle definition "begins with the initiation of therapy".

### 5. Monitoring in an unstimulated (natural-cycle) IUI
1. Who: a patient having IUI with ultrasound and blood monitoring but no ovulation-induction drugs.
2. Old: covered as part of the IUI cycle. New: covered under X1 through "Pelvic ultrasound" and "Blood tests". Unclear if "monitoring" in the exclusion is read to reach it.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Conflict X; X1 more plausible.
4. Text: NEW benefit "Pelvic ultrasound; ... Blood tests"; exclusion "Ovulation induction and monitoring".

### 6. Oral ovulation induction (non-menotropin)
1. Who: a patient whose ovulation is induced with an oral agent instead of menotropins.
2. Old: not covered under this benefit, which grants only cycles "with menotropins". New: covered as first-tier "Medically appropriate Treatment of ovulatory dysfunction", or as second-tier "Ovulation induction" under X1. Oral drugs fall outside the injectable exclusion.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Covered under X1 and under the first-tier reading; X2 would exclude "Ovulation induction" in any form. Covered is more plausible.
4. Text: OLD "Ovulation induction cycle(s) with menotropins". NEW "Medically appropriate Treatment of ovulatory dysfunction"; exclusion names only "Injectable" medication.

### 7. Patient who has already had 6 IUIs this policy year
1. Who: any eligible patient seeking a 7th or later insemination in the same policy year.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered (NEW sets no count), subject to medical necessity ("Clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent").
3. Reading-dependent: only through Conflict X (item 1).
4. Text: OLD schedule "Artificial insemination ... 6 attempts maximum per policy year". NEW: no count limit; Medically Necessary item 2 "frequency".

### 8. Number of menotropin ovulation induction cycles per year
1. Who: patients using many OI cycles in a year.
2. Old: covered with no stated cap. The 6-attempt cap names only "Artificial insemination", and "Each treatment type is counted as a separate cycle", so OI cycles do not use up IUI attempts. New: drug not covered at any count (item 2); services as in item 3.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. OLD: a minority reading counts a combined OI+IUI cycle against the cap. NEW: Conflict X.
4. Text: OLD "Each treatment type is counted as a separate cycle"; "Artificial insemination 6 attempts maximum".

### 9. Basic services not yet tried, where a less costly treatment clearly cannot work
1. Who: for example, a patient with severe male-factor infertility, or one who needs donor sperm, who goes straight to IUI after the diagnostic work-up without any first-tier treatment.
2. Old: covered (if the other gates are met), because the less-costly gate is satisfied when the cheaper route "cannot" succeed, and no sequence is required. New: covered under the more plausible reading; not covered until a first-tier treatment fails under the other.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Reading S1: "the above infertility services" are mostly diagnostic, so a completed work-up that shows no route to increased fertility meets the step. Reading S2: a first-tier treatment ("Treatment of ovulatory dysfunction", GIFT) must be tried and fail. S1 is more plausible, because diagnostics cannot "result in increased fertility" and GIFT is costlier and more invasive than IUI.
4. Text: NEW "If the above infertility services do not result in increased fertility". OLD "A successful pregnancy cannot be attained through less costly treatment".

### 10. A less costly treatment could still work, but first-tier treatment has already been tried and failed
1. Who: a patient whose first-tier treatment (for example ovulatory-dysfunction treatment) did not produce pregnancy, but for whom further cheaper attempts could still plausibly work.
2. Old: not covered (the less-costly gate fails). New: covered. Once first-tier services "do not result in increased fertility", NEW imposes no least-cost test. Medical-necessity criterion 3 bars services chosen "primarily for the economic benefit of Us", which cuts the other way.
3. Reading-dependent: yes, mildly. Some may read "Clinically appropriate, in terms of type" as a least-cost rule. The plain reading (no least-cost rule) is more plausible.
4. Text: OLD "A successful pregnancy cannot be attained through less costly treatment for which coverage is available under this plan". NEW Medically Necessary items 2 and 3.

### 11. A less costly treatment could work and no first-tier treatment has been tried
1. Who: a patient who wants IUI or a menotropin cycle while a first-tier option remains untried and promising.
2. Old: not covered. New: IUI not covered yet, because the first tier must first fail. The outcome is the same, but the ground has changed (a sequencing rule instead of a cost rule).
3. Reading-dependent: yes, through S1/S2 (item 9).
4. Text: as items 9 and 10.

### 12. IUI that meets the OLD gates but is not clinically appropriate
1. Who: for example, a patient who meets the table but has a finding that makes IUI ineffective (such as tubal blockage), or IUI at a frequency outside accepted practice.
2. Old: covered. OLD has no medical-necessity condition in the excerpt, and the less-costly gate does not bar ineffective treatment. New: not covered.
3. Reading-dependent: no.
4. Text: NEW "may be covered if the tests are determined to be Medically Necessary"; definition "considered effective for an illness"; "The fact that any particular Physician may prescribe ... does not, of itself, make the service ... Medically Necessary".

### 13. Treatment without prior approval
1. Who: any patient whose provider does not request advance approval.
2. Old: unclear, possibly not covered. OLD says the provider "will request approval from us in advance" but states no consequence. New: no prior-approval requirement in the excerpt, so covered.
3. Reading-dependent: yes, on the OLD side (procedural statement vs condition of payment). It is more plausibly a condition.
4. Text: OLD "Your provider will request approval from us in advance for your infertility services."

### 14. IUI or OI by a physician who is not an infertility specialist
1. Who: for example, insemination by a general obstetrician-gynaecologist.
2. Old: not covered (only "charges made by an infertility specialist"). New: covered if the physician is "qualified to provide such infertility services" under ACOG/ASRM.
3. Reading-dependent: no.
4. Text: OLD "We will cover charges made by an infertility specialist". NEW "All services must be provided by Physicians who are qualified".

### 15. Artificial insemination other than intrauterine (for example intracervical)
1. Who: a patient receiving a non-IUI insemination.
2. Old: unclear, lean not covered. The benefit lists only "Intrauterine insemination"; the schedule cap uses "Artificial insemination". New: covered (the benefit names "Artificial insemination"), under X1.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. OLD wording mismatch; NEW Conflict X.
4. Text: OLD "Intrauterine insemination" vs schedule "Artificial insemination". NEW "Artificial insemination".

## B. Who is eligible

### 16. Covered dependent child (not a spouse, civil union partner or domestic partner)
1. Who: an insured dependent who is not the student or the student's partner.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered (any "Insured Person who is an appropriate candidate").
3. Reading-dependent: no.
4. Text: OLD "You are covered under this plan as a student or as a covered dependent who is the student's legal spouse, civil union partner or domestic partner". NEW "Infertility services are provided to an Insured Person".

### 17. Infertility caused by a condition not "otherwise covered under this Certificate"
1. Who: a patient whose infertility results from a condition the certificate does not otherwise cover (for example an excluded condition, or sterilization whose reversal is excluded).
2. Old: covered if the gates are met (OLD has no such qualifier). New: not covered under one reading.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Q1: "otherwise covered" qualifies only "a correctable medical condition", so malformation, disease or dysfunction suffice on their own. Q2: it qualifies the whole list. Q1 is more plausible (the qualifier is attached to the last item, after "or a").
4. Text: NEW "the result of malformation, disease or dysfunction or a correctable medical condition otherwise covered under this Certificate".

### 18. Unexplained infertility (no identified cause) after 12 or 6 months
1. Who: a female (or couple) with no diagnosed cause after 12 months (female under 35) or 6 months (35+) of trying, meeting OLD FSH rules.
2. Old: covered, more plausibly. The table covers anyone meeting the months, but the "condition ... demonstrated to cause the illness of infertility" gate creates doubt. New: unclear.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Under C1 there is no demonstrated "malformation, disease or dysfunction", so not covered. Under C2, ASRM's third bullet makes this infertility, a "disease", and covered. I find this close. Lean covered, because unexplained infertility is presumed dysfunction and ASRM describes exactly this patient. A strict C1 reading would deny.
4. Text: NEW causation clause; ASRM "In patients having regular, unprotected intercourse and without any known etiology ... evaluation should be initiated at 12 months ... and at 6 months". OLD "There exists a condition that: Is demonstrated to cause the illness of infertility".

## C. Months of trying and partner type

### 19. Female under 35 with a male partner, diagnosed cause, under 12 months trying
1. Who: for example, 4 months of intercourse and a diagnosed cause (anovulation, documented male factor in the partner).
2. Old: not covered (needs 12+ months or 12+ self-paid donor cycles). New: covered (no waiting period when there is a known cause).
3. Reading-dependent: yes. Reading T1: ASRM's 12/6-month figures apply only "without any known etiology" and concern evaluation, not treatment. Reading T2: the plan uses those figures as a general waiting period. T1 is more plausible; ASRM's first bullet covers known-cause cases on "medical ... history ... diagnostic testing" alone.
4. Text: OLD table "A. 12 months or more or B. At least 12 cycles of donor insemination"; ASRM bullets 1 and 3.

### 20. Female 35 or older with a male partner, diagnosed cause, under 6 months trying
1. Who: as item 19, age 35+, fewer than 6 months or 6 donor cycles.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered.
3. Reading-dependent: yes (T1/T2; T1 more plausible).
4. Text: OLD "A. 6 months or more or B. At least 6 cycles of donor insemination"; ASRM bullet 1.

### 21. Mixed history: some months of intercourse plus some donor cycles, neither reaching its threshold
1. Who: for example, a female under 35 with a male partner, 6 months of intercourse plus 6 donor cycles.
2. Old: not covered (the table requires A or B; it does not add them together). New: covered if a cause is diagnosed; under C2 also covered without one.
3. Reading-dependent: yes (C1/C2 when there is no diagnosed cause).
4. Text: OLD "A. 12 months or more or B. At least 12 cycles of donor insemination".

### 22. Male patient with a female partner, documented male factor, under the months threshold
1. Who: an insured male of any age with a documented male factor, female partner under 35 with under 12 months, or 35+ with under 6 months. The IUI is performed on the partner, who must also be insured.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered. The partner-age-based waiting period disappears when the cause is known.
3. Reading-dependent: yes (T1/T2; T1 more plausible).
4. Text: OLD "A male of any age with a female partner under 35 years of age 12 months or more" / "35 years of age or older 6 months or more". ASRM bullets 1 and 3.

### 23. Female with a male partner who uses donor sperm because of the partner's male factor (for example no sperm)
1. Who: a female whose male partner has a diagnosed male-factor disease and who needs donor insemination.
2. Old: covered only after 12 (under 35) or 6 (35+) months of intercourse, or as many self-paid donor cycles, plus the FSH gates. New: covered without a waiting period. The partner's disease causes the couple's infertility, and ASRM counts the "need for ... donor gametes ... with a partner". Donor sperm itself is excluded in both versions.
3. Reading-dependent: yes, mildly. Whether a partner's disease satisfies "such infertility is the result of ... disease". ASRM's "with a partner" makes yes more plausible.
4. Text: OLD table option B, "Self paid/not paid for by plan"; NEW causation clause; ASRM bullet 2; both exclude donor sperm ("The purchase of ... donor sperm" / "Costs for an ovum donor or donor sperm").

### 24. Female under 35 without a male partner (single, or with a female partner), with a diagnosed medical cause, under 12 self-paid donor cycles
1. Who: for example, a single woman, or one with a female partner, with documented anovulation, who has had 0 to 11 self-paid donor inseminations.
2. Old: not covered (needs "At least 12 cycles of donor insemination", self-paid). New: covered. The medical cause meets C1, and ASRM forbids delay "regardless of relationship status or sexual orientation".
3. Reading-dependent: no in practice (C1 and C2 agree once a cause is diagnosed).
4. Text: OLD row "A female under 35 years of age without a male partner ... At least 12 cycles of donor insemination ... Self paid/not paid for by plan". ASRM bullet 2 and final sentence.

### 25. Female 35 or older without a male partner, diagnosed medical cause, under 6 self-paid donor cycles
1. Who: as item 24, age 35+, 0 to 5 self-paid donor cycles.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered.
3. Reading-dependent: no in practice.
4. Text: OLD row "A female 35 years of age or older without a male partner ... At least 6 cycles of donor insemination".

### 26. Female without a male partner, no diagnosed medical cause, who HAS completed the OLD donor-cycle count (12 if under 35, 6 if 35+)
1. Who: a single woman, or one with a female partner, with no identified medical condition, who has paid for the required donor cycles herself without pregnancy.
2. Old: covered. The table writes this patient in, and the glossary calls her "infertile" after those cycles. New: unclear, lean not covered.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. C1: no "malformation, disease or dysfunction" is shown, so not covered. C2: ASRM's bullets 1 and 2 and its no-delay sentence make her eligible. C1 is more plausible. One might also argue that the failed donor cycles themselves evidence "dysfunction" (as in item 18), which would favour coverage. **This is a possible loss of coverage.**
4. Text: OLD glossary "For a female without a male partner, after: At least 12 cycles of donor insemination if under the age of 35; 6 cycles ... if age 35 or older". NEW causation clause; ASRM bullet 2, "regardless of relationship status or sexual orientation".

### 27. Female without a male partner, no diagnosed cause, BELOW the OLD donor-cycle count
1. Who: a single woman, or one with a female partner, with no medical finding, seeking plan-paid donor IUI from the first cycle.
2. Old: not covered. New: not covered under C1 (more plausible); covered under C2.
3. Reading-dependent: yes (C1/C2). The most plausible outcome is unchanged, but the competing reading would extend coverage.
4. Text: as item 26.

### 28. Male patient without a female partner
1. Who: an insured male with no female partner (for example in a male couple), with at least 2 abnormal semen analyses at least 2 weeks apart.
2. Old: unclear. The glossary defines him as infertile, but the eligibility table has no row for him, and IUI would need a carrier, whose costs OLD excludes (item 29). Effectively not covered. New: IUI for him is possible only through an insured traditional surrogate (item 29); his own evaluation and semen analysis are first-tier.
3. Reading-dependent: yes (glossary vs table on the OLD side; surrogacy wording on the NEW side).
4. Text: OLD glossary "For a male without a female partner, after: At least 2 abnormal semen analyses obtained at least 2 weeks apart"; the table lists only "A male of any age with a female partner".

### 29. IUI of a traditional surrogate who is herself an Insured Person
1. Who: an insured woman inseminated (IUI) to carry a child for others, or the intended parent's IUI-related costs where the surrogate is insured.
2. Old: not covered ("All charges associated with: Surrogacy for you or the surrogate"). New: covered, because the surrogacy exclusion applies only "if the individual is not an Insured Person", subject to the other gates.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. "The individual" may mean the surrogate (more plausible) or the intended parent. Under either reading, costs are excluded when that person is uninsured. Whether an insured surrogate meets the causation clause is a further C1/C2 question; under C1 she must have her own disease or dysfunction, which makes coverage narrow in practice.
4. Text: OLD "Surrogacy for you or the surrogate. A surrogate is a female carrying her own genetically related child". NEW "Costs for and relating to surrogate motherhood if the individual is not an Insured Person under the Certificate".

## D. Age, FSH tests and dates

### 30. Most recent unmedicated day 3 FSH at or above 19 mIU/mL (any age)
1. Who: a female (any OLD female row) whose latest FSH is 19 or higher.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered if a qualifying cause exists and the treatment is Medically Necessary and appropriate; NEW has no FSH threshold.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. A high FSH may make IUI or OI "not considered effective" (Medically Necessary item 2) or the patient not an "appropriate candidate". This depends on the clinical case, not on a bright line. Lean covered where the physician documents effectiveness.
4. Text: OLD "Must be less than 19 mIU/mL in your most recent lab test". NEW: no FSH criterion.

### 31. No FSH test, an out-of-date test, or a test that was medicated or not on day 3
1. Who: a female under 35 whose last valid test is over 12 months old, or a female 35+ whose last valid test is over 6 months old; or anyone whose only test was medicated or not taken on day 3.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered (no testing requirement).
3. Reading-dependent: no.
4. Text: OLD "You need to have an unmedicated day 3 FSH test done within the past: 12 months" / "6 months".

### 32. Female 40 or older with any FSH result of 19 or higher after turning 40, even if the latest is lower
1. Who: for example, a 41-year-old with FSH 21 at 40 and FSH 12 last month.
2. Old: not covered ("in all prior tests performed after age 40"). New: no FSH rule. Covered if the cause is not purely age-related, or if age-related decline is accepted (item 33).
3. Reading-dependent: yes, through item 33's ageing question.
4. Text: OLD "If you are age 40 and older, must be less than 19 mIU/mL in all prior tests performed after age 40."

### 33. Infertility due to natural ageing (age-related ovarian insufficiency, perimenopause) in the patient
1. Who: a female whose infertility the physician attributes to age-related ovarian decline but who still ovulates or responds.
2. Old: not covered. New: unclear.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. A1: age-related ovarian insufficiency is ovarian "dysfunction", and ASRM expressly counts infertility "based on a patient's ... age", so covered. A2: a "natural physiologic process" is not "malformation, disease or dysfunction", so not covered. A1 is somewhat more plausible: NEW dropped OLD's express carve-out, and OLD itself called the state "ovarian insufficiency". Medical necessity still limits treatment that would not work.
4. Text: OLD "You or your partner does not have infertility that is due to a natural physiologic process such as age related ovarian insufficiency (e.g. perimenopause, menopause)." NEW causation clause; ASRM "based on a patient's medical, sexual, and reproductive history, age".

### 34. Menopause in the patient
1. Who: a postmenopausal female seeking IUI (with or without OI).
2. Old: not covered. New: not covered, more plausibly. IUI and OI cannot be "effective" without ovarian function, so they fail medical necessity and "appropriate candidate". The most plausible outcome is unchanged; the reason has changed.
3. Reading-dependent: yes (A1/A2 as in item 33), but medical necessity decides the outcome under either.
4. Text: OLD "(e.g. perimenopause, menopause)". NEW Medically Necessary item 2.

### 35. Natural ageing or menopause in the PARTNER, patient herself unaffected
1. Who: for example, a fertile female with a menopausal female partner, or with a male partner whose decline is age-related, where the patient is the one receiving IUI.
2. Old: not covered (the bar applies to "You or your partner"). New: covered if the patient's own eligibility is met (diagnosed cause, or a C2 reading); NEW has no partner-based ageing bar.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. If the only cause is the partner's ageing, C1/C2 and A1/A2 both matter. If the patient qualifies on her own, no.
4. Text: OLD "You or your partner does not have infertility that is due to a natural physiologic process".

## E. Voluntary sterilization

### 36. Patient's own voluntary sterilization, later reversed, with infertility now from another documented cause
1. Who: for example, a female with a reversed tubal ligation who now has documented anovulation.
2. Old: not covered ("with or without surgical reversal, regardless of post reversal results"). New: covered (no sterilization bar; the cause is a separate dysfunction).
3. Reading-dependent: no.
4. Text: OLD "You or your partner has not had a voluntary sterilization with or without surgical reversal, regardless of post reversal results." NEW excludes only the reversal procedures ("Reversal of tubal ligations; Reversal of vasectomies").

### 37. Partner's voluntary sterilization (for example vasectomy), patient has her own documented cause
1. Who: a female whose male partner had a vasectomy (reversed or not), and who has her own documented condition.
2. Old: not covered. New: covered.
3. Reading-dependent: no.
4. Text: as item 36.

### 38. Partner's voluntary sterilization is the only cause (for example donor IUI because the partner had a vasectomy)
1. Who: a female with no condition of her own, whose male partner had a voluntary vasectomy, seeking IUI (donor sperm, or partner sperm after reversal).
2. Old: not covered. New: unclear, lean not covered.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. C1: a voluntary procedure is not "malformation, disease or dysfunction", and reversal is excluded, so it is not a "correctable medical condition otherwise covered"; not covered. C2: ASRM counts the "need for ... donor gametes ... with a partner"; covered. Q1/Q2 (item 17) also bears on this. C1 is more plausible.
4. Text: OLD sterilization gate; NEW causation clause; NEW "Reversal of vasectomies" exclusion.

### 39. Patient's own unreversed or failed voluntary sterilization is the cause
1. Who: a female with tubal ligation (IUI cannot work) or a male with vasectomy (IUI with his sperm would need retrieval).
2. Old: not covered. New: not covered, more plausibly (causation clause; for tubal ligation, IUI would not be effective).
3. Reading-dependent: yes (C1/C2), but medical necessity defeats IUI after tubal ligation under either. The most plausible outcome is unchanged.
4. Text: OLD "This includes tubal ligation, hysterectomy and vasectomy only if obtained as a form of voluntary sterilization." NEW causation clause.

## F. Gender dysphoria

### 40. Patient or partner clinically diagnosed with gender dysphoria
1. Who: an insured patient who, or whose partner, has a gender dysphoria diagnosis, for example where gender-affirming treatment has impaired fertility, or where the partner cannot provide sperm or eggs.
2. Old: covered, more plausibly. The glossary makes the diagnosis itself a route to being "infertile" for "an individual or their partner", with no time requirement, though the table does not list this case. New: covered only where the infertility "is the result of malformation, disease or dysfunction". This is plausibly met where hormone therapy has caused a documented dysfunction. It is not met where the only basis is the diagnosis, or a partner's lack of the needed gamete with no dysfunction.
3. Reading-dependent: yes. C1/C2 on the NEW side. On the OLD side, glossary vs table (does the glossary route also need the table's months and FSH?). Lean: OLD covered; NEW covered only with a documented dysfunction.
4. Text: OLD glossary "For an individual or their partner who has been clinically diagnosed with gender dysphoria". NEW causation clause; ASRM "regardless of relationship status or sexual orientation" (this does not mention gender identity).

## G. Sperm source and handling

### 41. Thawing of frozen sperm (donor or the partner's banked sperm) for IUI
1. Who: any IUI using cryopreserved sperm.
2. Old: not covered ("Thawing of cryopreserved (frozen) eggs, sperm"). New: covered as part of artificial insemination under X1 for the partner's banked sperm. For donor sperm, unclear: "Costs for ... donor sperm" may or may not reach thaw charges.

### 42. Sperm obtained from a male partner who is not covered under the plan

## Internal tensions and contradictions

### Old version (Aetna 2023-24)
1. **Glossary vs eligibility table.** The glossary defines infertility for a "male without a female partner" (2 abnormal semen analyses) and for "an individual or their partner ... diagnosed with gender dysphoria". The eligibility table has no row for either, so it is unclear whether these people can ever meet "the requirement for the number of months".
2. **"Condition demonstrated to cause the illness of infertility" vs the table.** The gate demands a demonstrated causal condition, yet the table and glossary treat failure to conceive after a number of months or donor cycles as the disease itself. This creates doubt for unexplained infertility and for women without a male partner.
3. **"Your partner" is defined as a covered dependent spouse or partner**, but the table's "with/without a male partner" plainly refers to the relationship. A student with an uninsured male partner fits neither cleanly.
4. **Male rows set FSH to "Does not apply"**, while the eligibility bullet says "Your unmedicated day 3 FSH level meets the following criteria". Where both partners are insured, the female's FSH could be avoided by processing the claim through the male partner's eligibility.
5. **"Timed" vs "frequent" intercourse.** The table says "unprotected timed sexual intercourse"; the glossary says "frequent, unprotected heterosexual sexual intercourse". The two tests differ.
6. **Natural-ageing bar vs the explicit 40+ FSH rule.** The plan writes in a pathway for women 40 and older but bars infertility "due to a natural physiologic process such as age related ovarian insufficiency". It is unclear how infertility at 40+ is ever shown not to be age-related.
7. **"Intrauterine insemination" (benefit) vs "Artificial insemination" (schedule cap)**, and "attempts" (schedule) vs "cycle" (benefit). The scope of the cap and of the covered service do not match.
8. **Self-paid donor cycles vs the 6-attempt cap.** It is unclear whether the pre-eligibility self-paid cycles count toward the "6 attempts maximum per policy year". The natural reading counts only plan-paid ones.
9. **Donor-insemination pathway vs exclusions.** The table builds eligibility on donor insemination, while the exclusions remove donor sperm purchase and the thawing of frozen sperm. Covered donor IUI cycles therefore pay only for the procedure.
10. **"Charges made by an infertility specialist" vs "cycle(s) with menotropins".** Whether a pharmacy-dispensed menotropin drug is a specialist charge is unclear.
11. **"ART services" undefined.** It is unclear whether the "Obtaining sperm from a person not covered" exclusion reaches IUI.
12. **Prior approval** is stated as a procedure ("will request") with no stated consequence.
13. **Schedule:** out-of-network inpatient/outpatient is "Not covered", while the artificial-insemination row is "Not applicable" (outside the scope of this comparison, noted for completeness).

### New version (Wellfleet 2025-26)
1. **Benefit vs exclusion (Conflict X).** "Ovulation induction and monitoring" appears in the covered second tier and, word for word, in the Family Planning exclusions. "Gamete intrafallopian tube transfer" appears in the covered first tier and in the exclusion "In vitro fertilization, gamete intrafallopian tube transfers or zygote intrafallopian tube transfers". The exclusion's heading, "Infertility Treatment (male or female)", read literally, excludes the very benefit that item 6 grants.
2. **Treatments called "tests".** The second tier calls ovulation induction, laparoscopy, laparotomy and artificial insemination "additional tests", which then "may be covered if the tests are determined to be Medically Necessary".
3. **Duplicate listing.** "Pelvic ultrasound" appears in both the first and the second tier, so it is unclear whether it is gated by the "do not result in increased fertility" condition.
4. **Sequencing oddity.** GIFT (an invasive, IVF-type procedure) sits in the first tier, which must fail before the far less invasive IUI is reached.
5. **"Do not result in increased fertility" applied to diagnostics.** Most first-tier items (evaluation, semen analysis, biopsy, imaging) cannot increase fertility, so the trigger for the second tier is unclear.
6. **Causation clause vs the adopted ASRM guideline (Conflict C).** The policy limits coverage to infertility that "is the result of malformation, disease or dysfunction". ASRM defines infertility to include the "need for ... donor gametes ... either as an individual or with a partner" and says "Nothing in this definition shall be used to deny or delay treatment to any individual, regardless of relationship status or sexual orientation." The policy says it uses ASRM to determine eligibility, yet its own clause would deny the ASRM-defined group.
7. **Scope of "otherwise covered under this Certificate"**: whether it qualifies the whole causal list or only "a correctable medical condition".
8. **"May be covered ... if ... Medically Necessary"**: unclear whether "may" adds insurer discretion beyond the medical-necessity determination.
9. **Medical-necessity criterion 3** bars services chosen "primarily for the economic benefit of Us", which sits oddly with any implicit step-therapy reading of the tiered list.
10. **The ASRM guideline's time figures** govern when "evaluation should be initiated", not treatment eligibility, yet they are the only time thresholds the policy imports.
11. **Surrogacy exclusion wording**: "if the individual is not an Insured Person" does not say whether "the individual" is the surrogate or the intended parent.
12. **Fertility preservation drafting**: "provided when You;:" and "with or without a same sex partner;. and." are malformed. The benefit addresses same-sex partners while the infertility benefit's causation clause does not.
13. **Cross-reference mismatch**: the exclusions refer to the "Fertility Preservation Expense benefit", while the benefit is titled "Fertility Preservation Benefit" (minor).
